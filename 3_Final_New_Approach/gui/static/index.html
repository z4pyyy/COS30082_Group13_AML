        3. How it works

        Two separate image models recognise the crop and the disease. A fixed rule combines them into a score for each of the 333 pairs. An unseen pair (for example, a disease that was never photographed on this crop) can win, because each part was learned on its own.

        1. Encoder. DINOv2 ViT-L/14, pre-trained without labels on 142M images, cuts the photo into 256 patches of 14×14 px. The crop and the disease model each have their own copy, fully fine-tuned on DPD-Small train photos only.
        2. Pooling. The crop model uses the CLS token (a whole-leaf summary). The disease model uses attention pooling: it gives each patch a weight, and lesion patches get high weights. The heatmap in section 1 shows these weights.
        3. Heads. One linear layer gives 55 crop or 175 disease probabilities.
        4. Pair score. log p(crop) + log p(disease) + w × team ConvNeXt-T pair score, for all 333 pairs.
        5. Calibrate. Subtract b from the 317 pairs seen in training, so unseen pairs can win (calibrated stacking, Chao et al., 2016). Raw scores sent most unseen validation images to a seen pair, so calibration was needed.
        6. Rank. The highest score is Top-1. The 5 highest give Top-5.

        Evaluation (brief section 3.3, test set, scored once)

        H = harmonic mean of seen and unseen pair accuracy. "Disease alone" = disease model only, on unseen-pair test images: the harder check that the model learned the disease, not the crop.
